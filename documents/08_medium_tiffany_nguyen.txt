Nguyen (10.23.2025)    Press enter or click to view image in full size            Childhood Influences   Growing up in California, I always loved the serene palm trees, incredibly ambitious student culture, and the diversity embedded throughout my formative years, curriculum, and my hometown area.  Especially in the Bay Area, academic growth always remained a top priority. With such a high concentration of technology and startups in Northern California combined with the high presence of skilled labor, the children of immigrant parents were especially “expected” to perform well and excel to continually new levels of achievement.  (I can write a whole essay on this later)   California has an impressive selection of schools, both public and private available. Although my own parents each attended top UC’s, I began to formulate my own perceptions of what I wanted my own college experience to be.  For my Vietnamese immigrant parents, they had always expected their daughter to stay close to home by reasoning that “it wasn’t fit for a [Vietnamese] girl to go away from her parents.” I had experienced many cultural divides between my parents’ mainland Vietnamese ideals and my own more “individualistic” nature from growing up in America. However, I knew this battle was one that mattered tremendously.   College Admissions: Tiffany’s Choice   For many personal reasons and to carve my own educational path away from my parents’ pre-determined expectations and cultural norms, I found myself desperately fantasizing about a university experience away from home. Looking for a college that would accelerate authentic growth, freedom, and learning became my top objectives that I kept to myself.  After a nerve wracking college application season during the height of Covid-19, my top accepted choices were narrowed down to either Boston University or Northeastern University (plus a waitlist at Carnegie Mellon undergrad). I was ecstatic that both the Boston options fulfilled my dream of studying independently away from my home life.  Ultimately, I chose Northeastern and as a Class of 2025 alumni today, I am beyond grateful for this choice, my growth as a Husky, and the deep career exposure from the school’s robust co-op program. No university is “perfect”, and this digital article will summarize my personal Northeastern journey, my perception of the school’s strengths, as well as wishes for the institution’s executive leadership. I hope my perspective resonates with some current / prior students, faculty, or alumni!   Summary of my Northeastern Experience:   Reflecting holistically on my four years at Northeastern, I truly believe the school has a unique value proposition aimed at accelerating students’ exposure to career interests while also building tangible work experience.   Fall 2021 : It was my first time independently living away from home, and I was determined to maximize my outlets of growth through career oriented opportunities, social life, exploration of Boston, etc.   I moved across the country!  Joined a business chapter on-campus and made new friends :)  Started working with a professor as a project assistant (finance and strategy lead) to focus on Asian-American community initiatives!!    Spring 2022 : After being more situated in Boston, I decided to take on any additional business or leadership exposure. As someone with natural curiosity, I wholeheartedly believe the ability to not just seek but also place yourself in constantly new environments makes all the difference in long-term growth.   Served as a member on a client-facing consulting project  Joined my business chapter’s committee  Continued my work as a project assistant alongside my professor and gained more hands-on experience regarding Boston non-profits, strategy and execution, and interaction with various departments at Northeastern    Fall 2022 : With over a year of Northeastern experience up to this point, I met such a diverse array of students from around the world. I loved interacting with those from various different backgrounds, ethnicities, career interests, passions, etc. Similar to any institution, I met some of the most hardworking, driven students that only inspired me to push myself even harder. Sometimes, I also felt like pulling my hair from the more unresponsive, “cruising” type of students in group projects. One thing remained constant however: I was eager to gain even more leadership experience, hoping to build my exposure and readiness for my aspiring dreams of a future successful businesswoman. After all, I did choose a co-op school, targeted for career readiness.  And this semester, I…   Served as VP of Recruitment and Fundraising this term and led my first ever committee and“work stream” of meetings and deliverables under my focus  Continued and successfully executed a year-long event project for one of my favorite Vietnamese-American authors, Phuc Tran  Learned an incredible amount from Professor Garrett as a project assistant  Led a client-facing consulting team as a project manager and presented to a startup on our weekly deliverables  Fostered many more great friendships while learning the definition of “balance” along the way gradually   And at the beginning of October… I received my very first co-op offer! After advisor approval, I excitedly signed the offer and called my dad that I was moving to NYC next semester for my first finance co-op!   Spring 2023 : I had never even been to NYC, and this was to be my new home for the next 6 months?? Looking back, I am so grateful for my first co-op, as it taught me not just the valuable exposure to a global industry, but also refined the often underrated soft skills such as the ability to foster relationships with mentors, the willingness to “carve” your own brand no matter where you are, and the commitment to staying curious both at work and in your personal life.   And during this co-op term, I…    Worked full-time for 6 months in NYC at a financial services firm (Goldman Sachs NYC)  Attended extremely unique networking opportunities and hospitality events as a part of my specific co-op team experience  Fostered incredibly relationships with mentors that still actively inspire me today  Learned the initial ropes of “Corporate America” but there is always more learning here as well  Balanced business chapter commitments and battled ongoing health circumstances all at the same time  Continued my freelance photography and expanded my reach into a new city for new clients!!  I also did a fellowship the summer in between Spring and Fall 2023!    Fall 2023:  Being honest, this was an incredibly difficult semester for me due to many ongoing health circumstances. My mental health was drained from many of the prior month’s events, and I firsthand learned the importance of “protecting yourself” in the business and school community. Nevertheless, this semester was a blur, and I went through each week sometimes spending more time on my business chapter than my own school work for particular reasons.  This academic classes term, I…   Balanced heavy extracurriculars on top of a full-time course load!  Reconnected with friends I haven’t seen in a while since before my NYC co-op experience  Reflected on my future career path and my plans for the rest of my Northeastern journey :)    Spring 2024:  For many personal reasons, I wanted to leave Boston once more. I describe my college experience as “adventurous” and have even earned the nickname “miss worldwide” from my close friends for my Northeastern experience that seemed to feature a new location every semester. I never planned to be this spontaneous with my university plans, but looking back… that’s what makes life so fun sometimes. My adventurous spirit and quest to always feel as if I am growing can be attributed to both 1) my natural curiosity and 2) my strict Vietnamese parents that inversely made me feel that every new moment of “freedom” away from home should not be a moment wasted.   And so…    I moved to NYC again for a different client facing role at a financial services firm (Scotiabank NYC)  Explored new areas of the city and committed more deeply into my freelance photography (ongoing 6+ years today) as a creative outlet!  Visited friends occasionally and reflected on the type of career I wanted post-grad only to feel lost along the way  Joined an on-campus consulting club and a case competition to feed my newfound interest in the consulting industry  Received a summer analyst offer but ended up taking on a summer consulting internship in NYC for new exposure!    Fall 2024:  Only two terms left of my Northeastern journey, and I wanted to have no regrets when I graduate next spring. I had already committed myself to so much leadership and extracurriculars on top of a full-time course load and felt proud of my resilience. However, I found it difficult to separate my school identity from my personal identity. Was I … Tiffany from XYZ club? Was I … Tiffany a part of this organization? What happened to just being Tiffany… just being me? I remember coffee-chats were a personal highlight during my co-op experiences, as I loved learning about the stories and experiences of those around me. One associate on my team recounted his days of studying abroad in Spain and recalled that it was “one of the best times” of his life. And that coffee chat changed my priorities. I needed to start experiencing college for me again, instead of piling up my plate constantly with extracurriculars and new projects (even though career growth should always be a priority). I needed to balance myself again.   And this semester:    I moved to London full-time for a study abroad semester!  Visited over 8+ European countries during my abroad time through long weekends and breaks  Participated in a research project in London and met many new friends who accompanied me on those memory-filled travels.  Traveled solo to various countries and learned to enjoy the “quiet” and time to myself as crucial reflection periods.    Spring 2025:  My last undergraduate semester at Northeastern, how surreal. I had lived in Boston, NYC, London full-time at some point during my university experience, and I wouldn’t have done it any other way. My goals coming into university was about growth, exposure, and exploration, and I had done just that. And through various life and career experiences, I had to learn the hard way regarding strengthening my own boundaries and standards for friendships, my future aspirations, tbe type of people I want to surround myself with, etc.   And this last semester of Northeastern…    I officially GRADUATED! Exposure opportunity to various industries through co-op  2. Unique emphasis on non-traditional learning such as studying abroad and dialogues  3. Diverse and fulfilling student community niches   1. Exposure opportunity to various industries through co-op  You’re a business student? Northeastern’s co-op program has company types from startup to Fortune 500 companies across marketing, sales, finance, insurance, compliance, data, etc.  You’re a nursing student? Northeastern partners with various local hospitals, clinics, and labs.  You’re a computer science / engineering student? Google, Raytheon, IBM, Apple, etc are just some of the previous co-op employers.  As Northeastern students are able to commit to anywhere from 1–3 co-ops during their college experience, many students will use these experiences to either diversify their interests and experiment to see which role / company / industry best suited them or leverage the co-ops to very deeply learn about a particular focus. The opportunity to work at both small and large companies, apply your in-classroom knowledge to a real world setting, and network on the job all towards tangible resume experience is extremely valuable.  Personally, I worked in private wealth management, capital markets, and consulting during my undergraduate experience and learned so much about myself and my interests holistically. Many often pivot from their first jobs at a traditional four year institution, but at Northeastern… an affinity for experimentation within the student co-op culture is celebrated for a good reason.  2. Unique emphasis on non-traditional learning such as studying abroad and dialogues  As someone who grew up in a rather strict Asian household, the ongoing campus emphasis and opportunities to study abroad (semester-long), specifically a chance to participate in a “semester in” program elsewhere or through dialogues (shorter study abroad programs) felt surreal. I believe learning comes from a mix of both traditional classroom learning as well as non-traditional learning  (so Northeastern coded, I know),  and studying abroad provides immersive learning outlets for new cultures, exposures, and self-discovery.  From the semester in programs across London to Silicon Valley to the dialogues in Japan, Paris, or Italy to Northeastern’s sister partnerships, a good majority of my peers studied abroad. It was a “norm” at Northeastern, and I believe it helps balance out the often “competitive” recruiting culture for co-ops within the school environment.   3. Diverse and fulfilling student community niches   With over 25,000 undergraduate students, there are so many outlets and ways to get involved. From intramural sports to cultural clubs to greek life to club sports to business and career organizations to church youth groups to run clubs to the arts, I believe every student can eventually find their niche and community within Northeastern.  My club involvements across business chapters, a consulting group, and a photography concert magazine along with friendships from mutuals and class solidified my Northeastern experience. With such a big campus, it’s nice to find a “home” within campus.  Moving on to my own perception of the school’s weaknesses, these are definitely not exhaustive and I still unequivocally love Northeastern but hope they take their students’ feedback into account for their future trajectory and development.   My Personal Perception of the School’s Weaknesses:    Prioritization of short-term profit at the expense of school’s reputation  Lack of consistent support to strengthen Northeastern’s reputation and deep relationships to employers    1. Prioritization of short-term profit at the expense of school’s reputation    Lack of Affordable Housing Options On-Campus : According to many unofficial college forums, students, my own peers, and my own reflections on my college experience, Northeastern is a heavily “overenrolled” Boston campus. Although many universities across U.S. similarly face high enrollment and space issues, Northeastern continually increases their class size so much to the point where they even converted dorms meant for singles or doubles into triples in International Village (IV). This move earned the living assignments a nickname: “forced triple” or “forced double.” I have heard students reported their quality of life and mental health significantly decline from these accommodations. After visiting a forced triple myself this past year through a mutual, there was definitely barely enough room for every student to be able to study and “unwind” comfortably.   If not placed in a “forced” double or triple situation in IV, students also face a lack of affordable housing options on campus. On-campus dorms, suite style rooms, or apartment style are incredibly expensive and require an expensive meal plan if living arrangement are provided by the school, which brings me to my next point.  2. Lackluster Dining Hall Options (poor quality for the price students pay): I enrolled in a dining hall my first year of university, not because I wanted to but because I was required to do so. I vividly remember falling ill from the IV dorm salmon “sushi” and seeing pictures of students’ finding a rat on top of the IV kitchens. The pasta sauce was never “cooked” but instead simply jars of pasta dumped straight out of the can. Desserts always remained full, likely because unhealthy food is always cheaper to purchase in bulk.  There were always reports that the food quality mysteriously seemed to increase around family and friends weekend or holidays, but I would dread going to the dining hall for their lack of quality options. But during my freshman year without a kitchen, there was simply no other choice.  3. Over prioritization of a global campus: The sentiment around the Boston (main campus) of Northeastern is clear regarding this issue. Many students including myself believe that the executive leadership of Northeastern is focusing much too extensively on the “global campus” aspect instead of improving the campus quality and experience of the main Boston campus.  Why expand into Miami, Silicon Valley, Oakland, etc when students in Boston are constantly still expressing their concerns regarding lack of study space, dorms, advisor quality and turnover, capacity, etc? While the “global campus system” does have its merit for connections and diverse student learning (Silicon Valley and NYC campus for its proximity to companies), the other options often don’t feel as intentional to current students. Northeastern expanding into such a large number of locations often feels like a “money-grab” move to funnel as much tuition money instead of re-investing the money into areas students can truly witness and experience such as high quality advising, career network experiences, dorms, dining hall food quality, etc. This third point has also been seemingly hurting Northeastern’s external reputation, as the school is seen as “playing the game” to boost student enrollment, “gaming the rankings” and simulating “value” through expansion instead of creating additional true internal value of re-investing in students.   2. Lack of consistent support to strengthen Northeastern’s reputation and deep relationships to employers   I previously mentioned some impressive co-op partnerships such as Google, Apple, various financial services firms, startups, etc. These relationships Northeastern has been able to foster to continually offer co-op roles for years are definitely impressive, but spots at these “top” companies are anywhere from 1 to just a few per cycle.  And with over 25,000+ undergraduate students at Northeastern in 2024, this means co-op recruiting cycle is by nature… EXTREMELY competitive. Schools around the globe approach recruiting and career support differently.  Over my four years at Northeastern, my co-op advisor has changed 2–3 times and my academic advisor has additionally changed a separate 2–3 times. As my co-op advisor and career counselors did the best they could, and I am grateful for their time and support, I felt most of the “help” and “mentorship” actually came from peers that were upperclassmen within my business chapter and surrounding social circles.  Northeastern isn’t currently a “target” school and students often have to navigate the process of “recruiting”, “networking” and “summer analyst” preparation all by themselves. Without target school status, students have to exert incredibly high effort just to receive referrals and get their resume even considered before an “auto-reject.”  In my eyes, Northeastern is currently in that in-between status. If you worked extremely hard for an notable co-op in business for example, you can leverage this experience as a “stand-out” when it comes to summer analyst pre-networking and recruiting. However, if you are late to even realize the summer analyst timeline, it sometimes feels extremely difficult to catch up.  Relationships with employers isn’t just checking a box that ONE or TWO co-op spots exist per cycle, but it’s active communication to employers that Northeastern students have the resilience and drive to succeed at the company. It’s fostering continual relationships that build the Northeastern name for summer analyst and full-time roles, not just co-op.  For years, many students have urged Northeastern to explore additional consulting co-op exposure opportunities, but there additionally doesn’t seem to be an outlet where student feedback is taken in seriously.  My Personal Perception of the School’s Culture:  At Northeastern, you will find some people treat their college experience as a minimal checkbox, which is common at every institution. Additionally, you will meet some of the most hard working, entrepreneurial individuals who excel in co-op, are inspiring members of their community, engaged in how they can make an impact, and are incredibly studious all at the same time.  The people who choose Northeastern are generally very career-oriented since the school’s co-op program attracts already career focused individuals. I’ve been incredibly inspired by many of my peers here, and my co-op allowed me such immersive experiences into new cities, roles, focuses, and environments.  My Hopes for Northeastern’s Future:  I hope that Northeastern slowly realizes to truly listen to the feedback of their students. The co-op program is undoubtedly amazing, but it shouldn’t overshadow other pivotal aspects of the college experience such as academic class quality, dorm living, and access to career resources and support.  Recommendations to Northeastern    (My personal perspective and writing this as an alumni who cares):     Partner with companies to offer more consulting / strategy co-ops (so many consulting clubs and student demand for such roles, and only a few true “consulting” roles available  Prioritize ongoing RELATIONSHIPS (not just a co-op role) with companies to elevate Northeastern’s school status in recruiting (this will increase likelihood of summer internship and employment offers for students)  Deprioritize global campus expansion and address concerns of the Boston main campus first (housing, space, studying amenities, dorm food quality)  Don’t just “game the rankings” (even though all schools do). Northeastern has such a strong foundation already, it’s better to build trust with students and external parties through first building up academic rigor and consistent class quality, advisor quality, and employer relationships so that students feel valued first.
